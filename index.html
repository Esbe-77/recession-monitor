        How the score works

        Five pillars, weighted. Activity (GDP quarter on quarter and year on year, PMIs and their one-month change, industrial output) carries the most weight. Jobs and consumer covers the change in unemployment, consumer confidence and retail sales against inflation. Inflation and rates covers the inflation level and its acceleration, plus whether the central bank is hiking or about to. Fragility covers government debt and the current account. Oil exposure is an estimate of how hard the Gulf supply shock hits each economy, from its reliance on imported oil and on Hormuz routes.

        Each reading maps to 0 to 1 on a fixed scale (for example, a PMI of 52 scores 0 and 46 scores 1), and the score is the weighted average times 100. Levels: under 25 Low, 25 to 39 Elevated, 40 to 54 High, 55 and up Severe.

        Limits. Readings come from Trading Economics country pages, central-bank statements and market briefings read on 24 Nov 2026; some periods lag (Q2 GDP for several economies). The oil exposure and export shares are approximate structural estimates from pre-2026 trade patterns, not live figures. The score ranks relative stress; it is not a probability, and it is not investment advice.
